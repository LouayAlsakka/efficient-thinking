# ET-IV E2 — what the 3B must do if my mechanism is right. Written before it runs.

*Sautée, 2026-09-21 05:23Z. The 3B cache does not exist yet; no number below is measured.*

I claimed the Pareto collapse is driven by the composite `score`, via this mechanism:

> a policy that is BETTER at form produces more candidates that TIE on `valid` and `meter`, which
> leaves the composite as the sole discriminator and lets one candidate dominate everything.

Two policies agreed with it (7B 53.3% → 14B 60.0%, with form-invalid briefs falling 87 → 67). Two
points and a story is not much, and the story was written after seeing them. A third policy in the
OTHER direction is a real test, because the mechanism makes a **directional** prediction it can fail.

## The predictions

The 3B is the weakest of the three policies.

1. **Under the current dimension set, the 3B collapses LEAST: collapse(3B) < 53.3%.**
2. **The 3B has the MOST briefs with no form-valid candidate: > 87 of 300.**
3. Under **craft only (valid, meter)** the three policies stay close, near 28–29%, because that set
   is two booleans and its collapse is governed by ties rather than by policy strength.

## What failure would mean

- If the 3B collapses **more** than the 7B, the monotone trend is broken and "better form → more
  ties → more domination" is wrong or incomplete. I would withdraw the mechanism sentence and keep
  only the measured fact that the composite drives collapse — which stands on the dimension-set
  comparison alone and does not need the mechanism.
- If prediction 2 fails while 1 holds, the link I drew between form-validity and collapse is not the
  operative one, and I should stop asserting it.

The **ruling-relevant** result does not depend on any of this: removing the composite drops collapse
16.6–27.7 points at both policies measured so far, while policy moves it 6.7 the wrong way. This
test is about whether my explanation is right, not about whether the fix is.
