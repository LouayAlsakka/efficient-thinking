The challenge

Three papers, one claim, one command each. The author thinks he is right and would rather be shown wrong than believed.

This page is the review. There is no venue, no referee and no gate behind the Efficient Thinking series; there is a public record, a reproduction that fails loud, and this invitation. It is written by an engineer from outside the research community, working with AI assistance, and it says so on the front page.

The claim under attack. This series separates the intelligence of a frozen model from the experience it can use and the experience it can acquire, shows each is measurable on its own, and finds that a learning plateau can belong to acquisition alone. Every number behind it comes from one experimental system — a frozen 7B model on synthetic pipeline-debugging problems with a verifier — and the papers say "can" for that reason. Whether it holds elsewhere is exactly what this page asks you to find out.

The three results, one sentence each

VIIIA read-only linear head over a frozen model's own hidden state, fitted on its verified history, moves the quality–compute frontier: equal success at 2.3× less compute, +11 to +14 problems in 100 across four paired measurements of 300, and the model's own preference or a five-sample vote at the same decision do not reach it.paper

Published neighbours, as found

A first pass over about twenty-five abstracts, not a survey; the papers' §6a carries the reasoning. The closest objects to this series' instrument are OLIVIA (a frozen LLM's action choice as a linear bandit over its hidden state, learned online outside the model), HSRM (a small verifier-trained encoder over a frozen generator's hidden states) and Multi-Head Latent Control (control heads over frozen latent traces for a quality–cost trade-off); the closest to "selecting experience for a frozen agent" is ExpGraph (an external experience graph with utility-ranked retrieval); ReBalance shares the deep-state steering instrument for reasoning economy. The closest to the plateau is the echo-gap result on memory-based self-improvement; the closest to the lever are the scout paper (trained) and DORA (training-free); the baseline class is ExpeL. None of them separates using experience from acquiring it with the model and the mechanism held fixed. If you know work that does, that is the most valuable finding this page can receive: file it.

What you need

An Apple-silicon Mac with about 16 GB of free memory, Python 3.9 or later with mlx-lm (tested at 0.29.1 on mlx 0.29.3), and the repository. The model is Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in bf16, pulled on first use, about 15 GB. Nothing leaves your machine.

Where to aim

The places the author thinks the results are weakest, stated by him so you do not have to guess. A finding that lands on one of these is expected; one that lands elsewhere is more valuable.

VIII

  1. An alternative explanation for the matched-compute frontier shift that is not the head.
  2. Leakage in how the experience was constructed: any path by which a scored problem's answer reached the head.
  3. A flaw in the paired statistics, in particular the treatment of the loop's run-to-run variance.
  4. A reason the second search structure (SQL repair) does not establish that the mechanism transfers.

VIII-b

  1. A reading of the second-generation gain that survives the harness's own null replicates.
  2. A duplication instrument under which the redundancy figures come out materially different.
  3. A same-family disjoint-problem arm that does show new experience helping, at the same decision count.
  4. Evidence that the plateau is the head form's, not the policy's.

VIII-c

  1. The acquisition rule on a second task family: if "look before you commit" during collection does not move a head there, the claim is one family's.
  2. The exchange rate: 132 forced-look groups matched 290 of the second agent's within noise; a measured ratio, in either direction, would change the paper's §5.
  3. The probe: the paper claims the held-out proxy tracks the amount of experience and not its origin; a proxy that recovers the game ordering across the four heads falsifies that.
  4. The noise floor: every comparison is read against the largest within-arm range of the session at n = 300; a demonstration that this bar is too permissive, or that session drift between the 09-25 bracket and the 09-26 arms is larger than the base's within-day stability, moves the interval counts.

How review works here

  • Channel. Findings are filed as issues on the repository. Private correspondence goes to the address on the front page. The author answers, himself; no assistant replies in his name.
  • What a finding earns. A finding that breaks a claim is published on this page under the finder's name, dated, with the claim it breaks marked as withdrawn in the paper. The author decides what counts as breaking a claim, and says so in the issue with his reasoning, so a finder can argue with the decision rather than guess at it. The author runs any experiment the finder specifies, within the series' means, and publishes the result either way. Every accepted finding is thanked by name, with the finder's permission, in the paper it touches. No payment is offered, and none should be inferred.
  • Acknowledgement is not authorship. Authorship carries obligations that a web page cannot confer; nothing here makes anyone an author of these papers. An acknowledgement is also not an endorsement, in either direction: a finder's name beside a finding does not mean the finder endorses the series, and its presence here does not mean the series endorses the finder's other work.
  • Your words stay yours. Text you post as an issue is displayed there under the repository's terms; it is not assigned, and the author does not promise to incorporate anyone's words into a paper. What enters a paper is the finding, in the author's words, credited as above.
  • Nobody is named without permission. If you would rather be credited by handle, or not at all, say so in the issue.

Findings so far

None filed from outside yet. The series' own withdrawals are on the papers' pages and in the register, dated: a contaminated arm read 134 of 300 and was withdrawn; a bar computed without the arm under test moved a verdict from 2 of 3 to 1 of 3; a proxy ranked three heads backwards and was retired as a diagnostic. They are left visible because a challenge whose author hides his own failures is not one.
