Efficient Thinking

Can a fixed AI model become more capable without becoming larger?
Efficient Thinking measures how search, evaluation, feedback, experience, symmetry and representation change the useful capability obtained from a fixed model and a fixed compute budget.

What the series claims. This series separates the intelligence of a frozen model from the experience it can use and the experience it can acquire, shows each is measurable on its own, and finds that a learning plateau can belong to acquisition alone. The intelligence is held fixed by construction in every paper; experience is carried in a read-only channel outside the prompt; capability is verified externally and charged per unit of computation. The separation is demonstrated in one experimental system (VIII, VIII-b, VIII-c); whether it holds elsewhere is the question the reproduction route below exists to answer.

In plain words. A model that is never retrained can get better at a task by keeping a verified record of what it did before and reading it at each decision. Learning from its own past runs out quickly; learning from where another agent stood, or from a forced first look, goes further and restores the gain. The series measures each of these separately, on one system, and publishes the commands to reproduce them.

One question, from every angle: how much useful capability can a fixed intelligence get per unit of computation, and what moves that number? Each paper holds the model fixed, measures capability with an external verifier, and changes one thing: search, the evaluator, the judge, taste, feedback, labels, the internal ceiling, experience, symmetry, representation.

What each paper established, one sentence each:

  • I — search extracts what the evaluator already contains.
  • II — sampling saturates because errors correlate; only an external verifier lifts the ceiling.
  • III — how much judging you get per unit of compute is itself the capability.
  • VII — made to answer, the model says what its state knows; nothing is free inside.
  • VIII — verified history, read by a frozen head, moves the frontier.
  • VIII-b — a second generation of experience buys a small, consistent gain that does not separate from the loop's own noise; duplication revealed the saturation, and disjoint problems showed it was not the cause: the limit is the region of state space the policy keeps returning to (draft).
  • VIII-c — a frozen agent's experience plateau can arise from how it acquires experience, not from exhaustion of its ability to use it: the same head on states from where a second agent stood, or from a forced first look, restores gains the agent's own experience could not. v1.0.12 · md

The finding — Paper VIII-c

VIII is the setup; VIII-c is the finding. Same frozen model, same read-only head, 300 unseen problems. The base solves 22.0%. A head fitted on the agent's own experience reaches 26.8%, a gain that does not separate from base against the loop's own noise, and a second round of its own experience adds nothing separable (VIII-b). The same head fitted on experience recorded from where a second agent stood reaches 35.4%; fitted on experience from a forced first look before guessing, 41.2%. Nothing about the model or the head changed between those columns — only where the experience came from. That is the claim: a frozen agent's learning plateau can belong to acquisition alone. The head itself is not new: a small readout over a frozen model's hidden states, fitted on verified outcomes, is the same object as a value head or a learned verifier, and this series says so. What is new is what is held fixed and what is varied: the model and the head stay the same while only the source of the experience changes, which prompt-based memory cannot isolate, and which was not found in the value-function work read for the related-work pass.

The setup — Paper VIII

VIII — Experience, in three papers.

  • VIII · Utilization — verified experience improves a frozen model.
  • VIII-b · Saturation — more experience gathered by the same policy stops producing separable gains.
  • VIII-c · Acquisition — the plateau can arise from where experience is acquired; changing the acquisition policy restores useful gains.

Together, VIII to VIII-c separate three things usually called learning: fixed intelligence, the ability to use experience, and the ability to acquire experience worth using. Paper I began by asking how to spend an inference budget; VIII-c ends by asking how to spend an experience-acquisition budget.

Papers I–VII establish the frontier and its limits: search moves a fixed model along its quality–compute frontier, and only external information raises the ceiling. Paper VIII is the first paper in the series to move the frontier itself; VIII-c is what the series claims.

The setup, and the result to try to break in it. A frozen 7B model, given a read-only linear head fitted on its own verified history, solves more problems at the same inference compute — and, at equal quality, spends 2.3× less. The mechanism transfers to a second search structure with the head re-fitted. Whether a second generation of experience beats the first is being measured against the loop's own run-to-run noise, replicate against replicate, and is not claimed here. The headline reproduces from one command in about three hours on an Apple-silicon Mac.

ModelQwen2.5-7B-Instruct, bf16, weights never changed; the head is a linear layer read from one hidden layer (18) at one decision
TaskDebugging: 300 synthetic programs with an injected bug; the agent inspects, hypothesises a region, patches and runs the tests. Every task has a sibling region so the symptom cannot give the bug away
CapabilityProblems solved, decided by the test suite, never by a model. Paired per problem; 95% intervals from a paired bootstrap; McNemar on the discordant pairs
ComputeTotal tokens per episode with the controller's own inference charged; the matched control gives the base the head's token budget
"Verified history"The frozen model's own earlier episodes on other problems, labelled by the test suite — not by the model, not by a person. Only successes teach; the head is fitted once and never updated during the run
Second familyLlama-3.1-8B, weights never changed, its own head fitted from its own history: +20.0 points on the held-out slice, +14.3 [+7.7, +21.0] on a disjoint 300 (Paper VIII §7.4)
Second structureSQL query repair against a fixed schema; regions are clauses; the verifier is the result set. The head was re-fitted there from that environment's own history, so the mechanism transfers, not the weights

Review

The challenge page: the claim, one command per result, the four places the author thinks each paper is weakest, and the terms of review.

There is no venue, no referee and no gate. Review is public and runs through the repository's issues. The four places the author thinks Paper VIII-c is weakest, so you know where to aim: the acquisition rule on a second task family (if "look before you commit" during collection does not move a head there, the claim is one family's); the exchange rate between forced-look and second-agent experience, measured in either direction; a held-out proxy that recovers the game ordering across the four heads, which would falsify the claim that the proxy tracks amount and not origin; and the noise floor, if the within-arm bar is shown too permissive or session drift larger than the base's within-day stability. Paper VIII's four are on the challenge page. A fatal flaw is worth more than praise, and the author will run any experiment you name.
