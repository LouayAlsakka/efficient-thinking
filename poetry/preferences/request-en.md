# A request for twenty sentences about what you like in a poem

Thank you for considering this. We are studying whether a model can take on one person's taste in poetry — not
poetry in general, one person's. For that we need one thing from you, and it takes about half an hour, once: about
twenty short statements of what you want in a poem and what you cannot stand. Your own words, your own language,
any length from five words to a line. You will not be asked to rate anything, read anything a machine wrote, or
come back for a second session. In the study you appear under a pseudonym only.

There are no right answers and no need to be consistent. Contradictions are useful. The prompts below are only to
get you started; skip any, add any.

**What you want**
1. What has to be true of a poem for you to read it a second time?
2. Images: concrete or abstract, few or many, and what kind of image stays with you?
3. Sound: what do you want from rhythm and rhyme, and when should a poet break them?
4. Length: when is a poem too long, and when is it too short?
5. Endings: what should the last line do, and what should it never do?
6. Openings: how should a poem begin?
7. Feeling: how much should a poem say its feeling outright, and how much should it leave for the reader?
8. Subject: which subjects draw you and which bore you?
9. Voice: do you want to hear a person in the poem, and what kind of person?
10. Tradition: which poets or forms do you return to, and what do they do that others do not?

**What you cannot stand**
11. Name a habit in poems that makes you stop reading.
12. Words or images you consider worn out.
13. A kind of cleverness you dislike.
14. A kind of sincerity you dislike.
15. What makes a poem feel machine-made, generic, or "reverting to convention" to you?

**The hard part**
16. Two poems both correct in form and craft, and you prefer one strongly: what decides it?
17. A poem that breaks a rule you hold and you love it anyway: what did it do?
18. What is the difference, for you, between a poem that sounds like a poet and a poem that is that poet?
19. What would a poem written for you, by someone who knew your taste, get right first?
20. Anything else about your taste that the questions above did not reach.

If you are willing, one week after sending these, a single line naming which three of your statements matter most
would help; that is optional. Send the statements however is easiest: a message, a file, a voice note we transcribe.
